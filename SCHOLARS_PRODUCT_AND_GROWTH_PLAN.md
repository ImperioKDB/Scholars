# Scholars Product and Growth Plan

This plan translates the investment memorandum into measurable product work. It is intentionally staged so the team can learn before committing the full raise.

## Phase 0: Measurement foundation

**Goal:** know which users and channels create real value.

Build or confirm an internal dashboard for:

- registered users;
- profile completion;
- first match viewed;
- scholarship saved;
- application started;
- application status updated;
- deadline reminder opened;
- community contribution;
- seven-day and thirty-day return rates;
- acquisition source and campaign;
- cost per registered, completed, activated, and retained user.

**Definition of activated user:** a user who completes a profile and views at least one explained match within seven days. Confirm this definition before investor reporting.

**Output:** a weekly growth report that separates acquisition from activation and retention.

## Phase 1: Acquisition experiments

**Goal:** test channels before scaling spend.

Start with small, comparable tests:

- Meta campaigns with different messages;
- student referrals and shareable scholarship pages;
- a campus ambassador pilot;
- student-organisation partnerships.

Every channel gets a source identifier. Each campaign has a fixed budget, a hypothesis, a stop rule, and a review date.

Do not treat a registration as success. A campaign should be scaled only if acquired students complete profiles, view matches, save opportunities, or return.

## Phase 2: Catalogue trust and supply

**Goal:** grow catalogue breadth without lowering verification quality.

- Keep human approval as the publication gate.
- Use discovery automation to suggest candidate listings, not publish them.
- Track source, last checked date, deadline, evidence, reviewer, and correction history.
- Add a visible report/update path for students.
- Measure listing freshness and the percentage of active listings with current evidence.

## Phase 3: Provider validation

**Goal:** test willingness to pay before building a large provider platform.

Conduct structured interviews with scholarship providers and institutions. Test a lightweight offer first:

- verified programme page;
- applicant communication tools;
- application-intent signals;
- privacy-safe aggregate reporting.

Do not sell ranking placement. Keep matching independent and disclose any sponsored relationship.

**Evidence threshold before a larger build:** at least two organisations agree to a pilot with a defined use case, owner, and success measure.

## Phase 4: Investor reporting and data room

**Goal:** convert product activity into credible evidence.

Maintain:

- monthly user and activation trends;
- cohort retention;
- acquisition spend and channel performance;
- catalogue count and verification freshness;
- applications and deadline actions;
- provider conversations and pilots;
- infrastructure and legal costs;
- privacy and security controls;
- screenshots and approved testimonials.

The data room should distinguish measured results, targets, and assumptions.

## Product backlog by priority

### Must have before scaling acquisition

- Acquisition-source attribution.
- Completed-profile and activated-user events.
- Cohort retention view.
- Admin export of core metrics.
- Privacy-safe analytics definitions.
- Reliable listing freshness and verification status.

### Should have during the first 90 days

- Referral links for scholarship pages.
- Ambassador codes and a simple performance view.
- Campaign landing pages with clear value propositions.
- Provider interview tracker.
- Student feedback prompts after a match or application action.

### Later, after evidence

- Provider dashboard.
- Paid provider workflow.
- Aggregated applicant insights.
- Matching API.
- Expansion beyond Nigeria.

## Decision gates

At the end of each month, answer:

1. Which channel produces the cheapest activated users?
2. Which channel produces users who return?
3. Which product action predicts useful retention?
4. Is catalogue breadth or match quality the bigger constraint?
5. Is a provider willing to pay for a defined outcome?
6. What should be stopped?

The plan should be revised from evidence rather than from the original fundraising narrative.

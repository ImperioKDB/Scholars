## The opportunity in one page

Scholars helps Nigerian undergraduates find educational funding and related opportunities they can realistically pursue. A student builds one profile. Scholars compares that profile with structured scholarship requirements, explains the result, tracks deadlines, and helps the student organise applications.

The problem is practical: opportunity information is scattered across websites, WhatsApp groups, social posts, and PDFs. Requirements are inconsistent. Deadlines are easy to miss. Students spend time on awards they cannot qualify for, while outdated or unverified listings weaken trust.

The product is live. It currently has **216 registered users**, a catalogue of **60+ hand-verified scholarships and opportunities**, and **₦0 in paid marketing**. These figures should be refreshed from the dashboard immediately before this memorandum is shared.

We are seeking approximately **₦1,500,000** to complete formalisation and run a measured user-acquisition and validation programme. The objective is not to spend quickly. It is to test which channels create activated students, improve the catalogue and trust systems, and produce the evidence needed for a larger round or sustainable provider partnerships.

## Why this problem matters

A scholarship can affect whether a student continues university. Yet discovery is still fragmented, and a long list is not the same as a useful answer. Students need to know:

- whether they appear to meet an opportunity's requirements;
- what information or document is missing;
- when the deadline is approaching;
- whether the listing has been checked; and
- what to do next.

The National Universities Commission's 2019 statistical digest recorded **2,159,461 students at all levels in Nigerian universities** and **785,259 new undergraduate entrants** in that year. This is historical university-system context, not a current total-addressable-market forecast. Scholars is initially targeting a narrow, measurable segment of this broader population. [1]

## The product

A student creates a profile in about five minutes. Scholars then:

1. compares the profile with structured scholarship requirements;
2. ranks opportunities by fit;
3. explains which requirements match, do not match, or still need information;
4. lets the student save opportunities and track applications; and
5. provides deadline visibility and application-support tools.

Features live today include:

- eligibility-based matching with an explanation for each score;
- a dashboard for matches, saved awards, and deadlines;
- catalogue search and filters;
- an application tracker;
- AI-assisted personal-statement drafting that the student reviews before use;
- a document-readiness checklist;
- community discussions on scholarship pages;
- achievements and an installable mobile web app;
- fellowships, internships, and competitions alongside scholarships; and
- admin tools for checking and publishing listings.

## What is different

Scholars is student-first rather than provider-first. It is designed around the student's decision: **which opportunity is worth pursuing, and why?**

The early advantages are:

- **Local fit:** state and LGA of origin, JAMB and WAEC results, Nigerian institutions, and institution types are part of the model.
- **Trust:** listings are checked before publication, and matching shows its reasoning.
- **Workflow:** discovery, saving, deadline awareness, and application tracking sit in one place.
- **Low cost base:** the product has been built by a solo founder with no paid acquisition to date.

These are early advantages, not permanent barriers. Matching can be copied. The lasting advantage must come from verified data, student trust, usage evidence, and relationships with providers and universities.

## Business model to test

Students remain free during the audience-building phase. No revenue is being projected yet.

The first commercial tests should focus on provider-side value:

- publishing and programme-management tools;
- qualified applicant reach;
- privacy-safe, aggregated applicant insights; and
- partnerships or sponsorships that do not influence student rankings.

Providers should pay for tools and reach, never for a better match score. This separation is central to trust.

### Spending discipline

Funds should be released in tranches. The first acquisition tranche should answer three questions:

1. Can Scholars acquire students at an acceptable cost?
2. Do acquired students complete profiles and use matching?
3. Do they save, apply, return, or refer others?

A signup alone is not enough. The team should track cost per registered user, completed profile, activated user, and retained user.

## Risks and mitigation

- **Very early stage:** use staged spending and report leading indicators.
- **Single-founder dependence:** document processes and recruit advisors or collaborators.
- **No revenue yet:** test provider demand before building a large provider suite.
- **Manual verification:** use human approval as a quality gate while automation surfaces candidates.
- **Privacy risk:** minimise data, restrict access, maintain clear user controls, and obtain Nigerian privacy advice. NDPC guidance can bring organisations processing more than 200 data subjects in six months, or operating in listed sectors such as education, within major-importance requirements. [3]
- **Acquisition risk:** stop or change a channel when activation and retention do not justify more spend.
- **Competition:** build verified data, trust, and relationships rather than relying on matching alone.
- **Illiquid investment:** investors should invest only money they can afford to lose entirely.

## Long-term vision

Scholars starts as a scholarship matching platform. Over time, it can become an opportunity infrastructure layer connecting students, scholarship providers, universities, fellowships, internships, competitions, and other educational opportunities across Africa.

The long-term goal is simple: a qualified student should not miss a meaningful opportunity because it was difficult to find, understand, or organise.
